SOC battery research · University of Bath Private repo · paper in view

Teaching a neural network to read a battery's charge from sound.

figure · the LSTM cell · gates & state · drag to rotate

The problem

Lithium-ion state of charge is deceptively hard to measure. Coulomb counting drifts, the voltage curve sits flat for most of a discharge, and the ultrasound signal that does carry charge is tangled up in hysteresis, temperature and current. There is no clean mapping to invert.

The approach

Built from first principles: a NumPy MLP with backpropagation and a bias-corrected Adam optimiser derived and coded by hand, before any framework touched it. Then PyTorch RNN, GRU and a 200-step LSTM that fuse the acoustic features, time of flight, wave velocity and displacement, with voltage, current, temperature and coulomb counting. Memory is the point: the LSTM resolves the charge-discharge hysteresis a memoryless model physically cannot.

The result

On real EDF Paris bus data it holds a sealed-test error of 0.57%, and 0.84% on the public US06 cycle, both sealed from training and from model selection, the protocol most published work quietly skips. The open problem I am on now: generalising cleanly from controlled cycles to messy real-world mission profiles.
