Internally peer-reviewed · for Energy and AI

Battery ML research · first author

Reading a battery's charge from sound.

Coulomb counting drifts and the voltage curve sits flat. So I taught an LSTM to read state of charge from ultrasound and cell swelling, tested blind on real drive cycles.

  • 0.57% mean absolute error on a blind real-world Paris electric-bus cycle.
  • 42% less error on US06 once ultrasound and cell swelling join voltage, current and temperature.
  • A fully blind protocol: one real vehicle recording held out of every step, model selection included.
Details
  • US06: 1.36% down to 0.79%, better on all ten of ten seeds (p = 0.002).
  • Blind Paris: 0.70% down to 0.57% for the same fusion, a 19% cut.
  • Built from first principles: a NumPy network with hand-derived backpropagation and Adam, then PyTorch LSTM ablations over features and architectures.
  • Python
  • NumPy
  • PyTorch
  • LSTM
  • feature ablation
  • 10-seed blind test

Year 2 design · snowmobile drive shaft

From 20kW at the motor to 210Nm at the track.

An electric snowmobile puts 20kW through one shaft at −40°C. I own the loads, free-body diagrams and drive ratio, built as a live calculation engine.

  • 3.294:1 by toothed belt, down to 209.7Nm at 910.7RPM, sized for 50km/h on a 290mm track sprocket.
  • A symbolic loads engine: change one input and every reaction, moment and stress updates, with 39 automated checks.
  • Final design locked: 431 stainless at a safety factor of 6.7, 4.35kg on Fenner Taper Lock hubs.
Details
  • Configuration B puts the belt pulley between the bearings and cuts peak bending by 56%, from 110.0Nm to 47.9Nm, with bearing reactions of 658.4N and 798.7N.
  • Bearings sized for a 5200h life, 284 million revolutions at shaft speed; the SKF 6006 and 6007 exceed it more than 30 times.
