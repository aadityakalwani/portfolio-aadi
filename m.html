Battery ML research · first authorPaper in preparation · target: Energy and AI

Reading a battery's charge from sound.

Coulomb counting drifts, and the voltage curve sits flat for most of a discharge. So I fused ultrasonic time of flight and cell swelling with voltage, current and temperature, and trained an LSTM to read state of charge on real automotive duty cycles, with the test cycle locked away from training, validation and model selection alike.

0.57%mean absolute error on a blind real-world Paris electric-bus cycle, ten-seed mean
42%less error on the US06 cycle once ultrasound joins the inputs, 1.36% down to 0.79%, holding on all ten of ten seeds (p = 0.002)
19%less error on blind Paris for the same fusion, 0.70% down to 0.57%

A fully blind protocol. A whole real vehicle recording held out from every step of the workflow, model selection included. The paper's positioning table finds no prior study that does the same.

Built from first principles. A NumPy network with hand-derived backpropagation and a bias-corrected Adam optimiser before any framework, then PyTorch RNN, GRU and LSTM ablations.

Feature ablation, blind test. Mean absolute error (percent, mean and standard deviation over ten seeds) and the share of seeds that beat the baseline.

Winkit · local food delivery · co-founder and CTOLive · winkit.uk

A delivery platform, co-founded and built end to end.

The aggregators wanted 38 to 40% of a local kitchen's revenue to deliver for it. So three of us started our own, and I built every line of the technical stack: a customer app on live Stripe payments, adapters that land each order on the kitchen's own system, and a separate driver app.

Three zeros for the kitchen: £0 commission (the kitchen keeps its own menu price), 0 drivers to hire, 0 new hardware.

Orders land on kitchens' existing systems: per-kitchen adapters have pushed live orders onto two different restaurant platforms, no extra tablet on the counter.

Live Stripe card payments on winkit.uk, gated to each kitchen's real opening hours.

A separate driver app: sign-up, shifts, live location, GPS dispatch and real-time order state, on Neon Postgres.

Run lean on free tiers: I traced a cron keeping the database awake 24/7 and gated it by opening hours, cutting compute burn by about 68%.

One order, five stations: customer pays, the kitchen's own system, ready, dispatched, delivered. Orders land on the kitchen's own system, so the counter needs no extra tablet.

Year 2 design · snowmobile drive shaftIn progress · stage gate October 2026

From 20kW at the motor to 209Nm at the track.

An electric snowmobile has to put 20kW through a shaft small enough to sit between two track sprockets, survive -40°C slush and steam cleaning, and come apart with standard tools. My half of our pair: the free-body diagrams, the loads and the drive ratio, built as an engine rather than a page of hand sums.

20kW at 3000RPM in, stepped down 3.28:1 to a 915RPM shaft carrying 209Nm, sized for 50km/h on a 290mm track sprocket.

A symbolic loads engine: every reaction, moment and stress flows from named inputs, so a design change ripples through instantly.

39 automated assertions pass on the calculation core.

Critical section located: an 80.4Nm resultant bending moment at the drive sprocket, bearing reactions of 590.6N and 1340.1N.

Stiffness checked, not assumed: 106µm peak deflection, bearing slopes of 2.26 and 3.65 arcmin.

Bearings sized for a 5200h life, ten hours a week for ten years, about 285 million revolutions at shaft speed.

Motor 3000RPM · 63.7Nm to 3.28:1 to Shaft 915RPM · 209Nm.
